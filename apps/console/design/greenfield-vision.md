# Greenfield Vision — The Agentic Experience for a Governed Credit-Risk Analyst

**A clean-room design produced by a three-lens panel (Airbnb-school trust design · Anthropic-school AI-experience research · Apple-school universal comprehensibility), built to Eastern-European engineering standards, for American enterprise users.**

*Inputs: the problem statement and the open web only. No existing design work in this repository was consulted.*

---

## 0. Executive Summary

**The surface decision.** The primary surface is **not a chat app**. It is an **Inquiry workspace** in which the unit of work is a durable, addressable answer artifact — the **Brief** — and conversation is the steering mechanism, not the product. Chat is how you drive; the Brief is what you keep. A thread pane steers the agent; the Brief pane accumulates the answer as structured claims, each claim carrying its own evidence chip; a Work log records what the agent actually did; a Ledger records every authorized execution. One product, three projections: the analyst lives in the thread and mechanics, the executive reads the Brief top sheet, the compliance reviewer reads the Ledger — all views of the same object, never three products.

**Why:** in a domain where a wrong-but-confident answer is worse than no answer, the transcript is the wrong system of record. Transcripts scroll; evidence must not. Chat-first agent UX is now a documented failure pattern ([Hatchworks](https://hatchworks.com/blog/ai-agents/agent-ux-patterns/), [Wattenberger](https://wattenberger.com/thoughts/boo-chatbots/)), and the strongest 2025-26 products — Gemini/ChatGPT deep research, Hex, Cursor — have all quietly moved the center of gravity from the message to the plan, the artifact, and the diff.

**The seven defining moments** (each fully specified in §4.3):

1. **First contact (minute zero).** No tour, no manual. The empty workspace ships with three completed example Briefs against the bank's real semantic layer; hovering one evidence chip teaches the entire trust system. The system invites: *"Ask something you already know the answer to."*
2. **The wait (30–90 seconds).** Never a spinner, never fake progress. A live plan with real tool events ("Read schema: LOAN_MASTER — 84 columns"), findings pinning as they land, an honest elapsed timer, and a first-class *leave-and-return* path. Operational transparency, but only of real operations.
3. **The answer lands.** The Brief composes **atomically** — we never stream the answer prose. A claim cannot render before its evidence is bound. One-second read (headline + chips), ten-second read (claims), ten-minute read (SQL, lineage, provenance).
4. **The Gate (authorization ceremony).** Warehouse execution is a signing moment, not a confirm dialog: plain-English intent, the editable SQL, cost/rows/cap, named policy checks passed, and the ledger notice ("this run is recorded"). A deliberate keyboard chord, never Enter. Scoped session consent instead of per-query nagging.
5. **The refusal.** Institutional, not personal: what was blocked, the named machine-enforced policy (linked), and always the compliant alternative, one click away. The rail lives below the model, and the UI says so.
6. **The repair.** Every claim carries a challenge affordance. When challenged or wrong, the agent proposes the verification query rather than defending itself; accepted corrections are promoted (with attribution) into curated knowledge. Trust is built at repair, not at success.
7. **The handoff.** A Brief exported to a memo, deck, or email carries its evidence chips, its "How we know this" appendix, and its ledger stamp. **No naked numbers leave the building.** The exec view is a projection of the same Brief, never a dumbed-down second product.

**The trust language in one paragraph.** Four fixed words, used identically everywhere, mapped to the substrate's calibrated levels: **Corroborated** (independent source classes agree) · **Sourced** (one authoritative source) · **Reported** (observed in profiling or analyst usage, not authoritative) · **Inferred** (AI belief, no independent source). Chips encode the level as word + fill-level glyph (never color alone; survives grayscale screenshots). **No percentage confidence anywhere** — verbalized LLM confidence is systematically miscalibrated, and false precision is a lie with decimals. The agent's prose stays neutral; confidence lives only in the chips. "I don't know — and here is the query that would find out" is a designed, respectable answer state.

**The three contrarian bets.** (1) Never stream the answer; stream the work. (2) No numeric confidence, ever. (3) The product's success metric includes users *declining* to rely on weak claims — we optimize calibrated trust, not maximal trust, and we will ship planted-error tests as a release gate.

## 1. Landscape: the 2025–26 State of the Art, and the Named Failure Modes

*Method: web survey across consumer agents, professional/agentic tools, enterprise copilots, trust-calibration science, and adjacent high-stakes traditions (aviation, clinical decision support, terminal-class finance tools). Patterns are named and cited; failure modes likewise. Full source list in §7.*

### 1.1 Steering and waiting: how agents take direction and spend time

- **Plan-before-work.** Gemini Deep Research presents an editable research plan the user can prune and constrain before execution begins ([Google](https://gemini.google/overview/deep-research/)); ChatGPT's deep research instead front-loads clarifying questions before its long run ([LivePlan comparison](https://www.liveplan.com/blog/planning/deep-research-chatgpt-vs-gemini)). Both concede the same point: for multi-minute agent work, the cheap human intervention belongs *before* the spend, not after.
- **Async by default.** Deep-research products let you "quite literally turn off your computer" and get notified on completion ([Google AI docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deep-research)); 3–15 minute latencies make waiting-state design "non-negotiable" ([philschmid](https://www.philschmid.de/deep-research-update)). The chat-holds-you-hostage model is dead for real work.
- **Reasoning disclosure, collapsed.** Claude and the ChatGPT o-series expose a labeled, collapsed-by-default reasoning phase; current Claude models summarize thinking rather than exposing raw token streams ([Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/extended-thinking); [Digestible UX teardown](https://www.digestibleux.com/p/how-ai-models-show-their-reasoning)). The emerging etiquette: label the phase honestly ("Searching…", "Reading schema…"), keep it out of the way, make it available.
- **The critical caveat — reasoning traces are not explanations.** Anthropic's own research shows chain-of-thought is often unfaithful: models acknowledged decisive hints in only ~25% of relevant cases, and Anthropic describes the thinking trace as a scratchpad that *influences* output rather than a faithful account of computation ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-faithfulness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoning); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/dont-believe-reasoning-models-chains-of-thought-says-anthropic)). Any UI that presents CoT as "why the AI said this" is committing explainability theater. What *is* audit-true: the tool calls, the retrieved records, the executed SQL.
- **The labor illusion — used honestly.** Buell & Norton showed operational transparency during waits increases perceived value and can even beat instant results; perceived wait time shrinks when real work is shown ([Management Science 2011](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1376)). The 2025 agent products that feel trustworthy during long runs are the ones showing real steps. The corollary under engineering honesty: show *actual* operations or show nothing.

### 1.2 Evidence and trust surfaces: citations, confidence, grounding

- **Claim-level citation is the winning pattern.** Perplexity's layered model — inline numbered citations, hover metadata, expandable source list — lets users choose 2-second verification or deep inspection ([AI UX Playground teardown](https://aiuxplayground.com/teardowns/perplexity/output/); [Shape of AI: Citations](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/citations)). NN/g's guidance sharpens it: place the source *next to the specific claim it supports*, not at the end of the answer — and beware that citations themselves can be hallucinated, so citations must be system-bound references, not model-generated strings ([NN/g on hallucinations](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-hallucinations/)).
- **Calibrating trust, not building trust.** Google's People + AI Guidebook frames the goal as helping users know *when* to trust and when to prioritize their own judgment, and treats confidence displays as double-edged: they can help calibration or mislead, depending on form ([PAIR: Explainability + Trust](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)). The canonical theory is Lee & See's trust-calibration model — design for *appropriate reliance*, where trust matches actual capability ([Human Factors, 2004](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392)).
- **Verbalized confidence is broken.** Multiple 2024–26 studies find LLM verbalized confidence systematically overconfident; RLHF and instruction tuning degrade calibration; expected calibration error of 0.05–0.20 means "90% confident" may be right 70–85% of the time; mechanistic work suggests verbalized confidence is a near-orthogonal "readout" from whatever calibration the model internally encodes ([Groot & Valdenegro-Toro](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02917); [Taming Overconfidence in RLHF](https://openreview.net/forum?id=l0tg0jzsdL); [Nature Machine Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01217-9)). Design consequence: numeric self-reported confidence is not a UI primitive. Our substrate's confidence is different in kind — it is *derived from evidence structure* (how many independent sources corroborate), which is exactly the sort of confidence that can honestly be displayed.
- **Grounded semantic layers are the accuracy story.** Vendor benchmarks put raw one-shot GPT-4o at ~51% on enterprise text-to-SQL versus 90%+ when grounded in a governed semantic model (Snowflake Cortex Analyst — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/data-infrastructure/snowflake-launches-cortex-analyst-an-agentic-ai-system-for-accurate-data-analytics)); Databricks reports a similar 32%→90%+ arc for Genie. Vendor numbers, but directionally consistent: the substrate this product sits on is the right one, and the UI's job is to make that grounding *legible*.
- **The enterprise counterexample.** Microsoft's own Power BI Copilot documentation concedes it "can produce inaccurate or low-quality outputs" and is "nondeterministic," with community-documented failures like silently answering from report visuals ([Microsoft Learn, via Colrows analysis](https://colrows.com/blogs/power-bi-copilot-wrong-answers/)). The analysis notes the regulatory kicker for banks: a system that cannot reproduce an answer or hand an auditor the exact query behind a number is structurally hard to defend under BCBS 239 or SOX. Our product's execution ledger is not a compliance tax — it is the moat.
- **Hex's convergent design.** Hex's Fall 2025 platform pairs a Notebook Agent (deep work) with Threads (conversational self-serve), both grounded in semantic models and endorsed tables ([Hex](https://hex.tech/blog/fall-2025-launch/)). Their tagline — "where trust meets insight" — signals the market has realized trust *is* the product. Their architecture validates our two-audience/one-substrate approach while their notebook-centricity marks where we diverge (§2).

### 1.3 Authorization and audit: permission models and ledgers

- **Tiered autonomy is the consensus.** The pattern language emerging for agent consent: **auto-approve** safe reversible actions (reads, searches), **notify** for recoverable actions, **block-and-wait** for irreversible or high-stakes ones — with explicit specification of who approves, what context they see, and how approvals are logged ([Smashing Magazine, Feb 2026](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/); [Agentic Design patterns](https://agentic-design.ai/patterns/ui-ux-patterns)).
- **Per-action prompting fatigues; batch review is demanded.** Claude Code's incremental yes/yes-always/no model is precise but users publicly request Cursor-style batch diff review — do the work, then review the whole change set at once ([claude-code#31888](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/31888)). The lesson for our Gate: granularity of consent must match granularity of *risk*, not granularity of action.
- **Session tracing is now table stakes.** Salesforce's Agentforce logs every interaction — inputs, responses, reasoning steps, guardrail checks — into a queryable Session Tracing Data Model with an observability console ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/observability/)). Enterprise agents are expected to come with flight recorders.
- **Regulatory tailwind.** EU AI Act Article 14 requires high-risk AI systems be designed for effective human oversight, including — remarkably — that overseers "remain aware of automation bias" (Art. 14(4)(b)) and retain the ability to disregard or interrupt output ([AI Act text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)). A US bank isn't directly bound here, but the regulatory direction of travel matches our design instincts exactly: oversight must be *designed in*, and the UI itself must fight overtrust.

### 1.4 Adjacent traditions worth stealing from

- **Aviation — the quiet, dark cockpit.** Airbus's philosophy since the A310: when everything is normal, nothing is lit; annunciate only the abnormal, with a central fault system prioritizing what needs attention now ([Airflow blog](https://www.airflow.blog/2025/01/16/the-dark-cockpit-philosophy-enhancing-efficiency-and-safety-in-modern-aviation/)). And modern flight decks pair every alert with a *procedure* — the ECAM shows the fault and the checklist. Steal both: notify only on state that demands human action, and never surface a problem (or a refusal, or a low-confidence flag) without the next move attached.
- **Clinical decision support — alert fatigue is lethal.** CDS override rates run 90–95%, largely from low-relevance, badly timed alerts; the "Five Rights" framework (right information, right person, right format, right channel, right time) is the countermeasure ([JMIR systematic review](https://medinform.jmir.org/2020/7/e15653); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9754301/)). Windows UAC is the same failure in consumer clothing — prompts that reduce to "do you want to do the thing you just said?" protect against nothing and train click-through ([BeyondTrust](https://www.beyondtrust.com/blog/entry/user-account-control-best-practices); [Schneier on habituation](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2018/06/the_habituation.html)). Our Gate must be rare, information-dense, and worth reading — or it will be blindly clicked within a month.
- **Terminal-class finance tools — density is earned.** The Bloomberg Terminal's design serves professionals who act on huge volumes in seconds: information density, keyboard primacy, minimal latency; its designers "conceal complexity" rather than remove capability ([UX Magazine](https://uxmag.com/articles/the-impossible-bloomberg-makeover); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/stories/how-bloomberg-terminal-ux-designers-conceal-complexity/)). The command palette (Cmd+K) tradition — Superhuman, Linear — shows how a modern tool teaches its own shortcuts and makes speed the product ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-to-build-a-remarkable-command-palette/)).
- **Airbnb — trust between strangers is a designed system.** Gebbia's account: reputation systems beat demographic similarity once reviews accumulate ("high reputation beats high similarity" past ~10 reviews), and disclosure has a designed *right amount* — too little or too much both collapse acceptance ([TED](https://www.ted.com/talks/joe_gebbia_how_airbnb_designs_for_trust)). Translation: trust between a human and an AI stranger is also buildable from structured disclosure at the right dose — which is precisely what an evidence chip is.
- **Human-AI interaction canon.** Microsoft's 18 guidelines (make clear what the system can do and *how well*; support efficient correction; scoped services) remain the most-cited practitioner framework ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-design/)); Dietvorst's algorithm-aversion result — people abandon algorithms after one visible error *unless given even slight ability to modify them* — is the single most actionable finding for our SQL-editing Gate ([Dietvorst, Simmons & Massey 2015](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2466040)).

## 2. Questioning the Default Surface

The reflex answer in 2026 is "it's an AI product, so it's a chat window." The panel interrogated that reflex against five honest alternatives before committing.

### 2.1 The candidates, honestly

**A. Pure chat (ChatGPT-pattern).** *For:* zero learning curve — the one AI mental model every American enterprise user now has; maximal flexibility; cheapest to build. *Against:* the transcript is a linear, ephemeral log — answers scroll away, evidence gets buried, there is no addressable object for audit or handoff; it puts the burden of interface on the user's prompting skill ([Wattenberger](https://wattenberger.com/thoughts/boo-chatbots/)); it structurally invites anthropomorphism (F3) and makes the exec's 10-second read impossible. NN/g's finding that users of task chatbots want "quick answers, not a conversation" applies doubly to a VP.

**B. Notebook (Hex/Jupyter-pattern).** *For:* computational narrative; reproducibility; the strongest substrate for deep analyst work — Hex's Notebook Agent proves agents thrive here ([Hex](https://hex.tech/blog/introducing-notebook-agent/)). *Against:* our analysts are credit-risk analysts, not notebook-native data scientists; notebooks center *computation*, but this product's center is *assertion with evidence* — most sessions never need a code cell; and a notebook is alien territory for the executive audience. Hex itself had to bolt on Threads for non-technical users — evidence that the notebook cannot be the shared primary surface.

**C. Dashboard-with-an-agent (Power BI/ThoughtSpot-pattern).** *For:* execs already live in dashboards; consumption is effortless. *Against:* dashboards presuppose you know the questions; this product exists for *investigation of unknowns* (what does this table mean, can I trust this metric, where does this number come from). The pattern's flagship implementation ships with a documented wrong-answer problem (F7). Dashboards are a fine *output* of our system (pinned live Briefs, v2) — not its heart.

**D. Command palette / omnibox only (Superhuman/Linear-pattern).** *For:* analyst speed; keyboard-first; instant entity lookup. *Against:* a palette is a launcher, not a place — answers need somewhere durable to live; multi-step agent work needs a visible plan and a visible work log. The palette is the *ignition*, not the vehicle.

**E. Report-first (deep-research pattern: request → artifact).** *For:* matches the async reality of tens-of-seconds work; produces exactly the durable artifact we want; the exec-friendly form. *Against:* pure fire-and-forget forfeits mid-flight steering — and analyst work is iterative: clarify, redirect, drill, authorize. Gemini and ChatGPT both had to re-insert conversation (editable plans, clarifying questions) in front of their long runs.

### 2.2 The decision

> **Primary surface: the Inquiry workspace — an answer-artifact editor steered by conversation.**
> Each **Inquiry** is a case file containing a **thread** (steering), a **Brief** (the accumulating, claim-structured answer artifact), a **Work log** (the agent's real actions), and its slice of the **Ledger** (authorized executions). The thread pane and Brief pane sit side by side; a global command bar (⌘K) is the ignition from anywhere.

This is E's artifact spine with A's steering wheel, D's ignition, and B's mechanics available on demand — under one discipline: **everything durable lives in the Brief; everything transient lives in the thread.**

### 2.3 Defense against the runners-up

- **Versus pure chat:** trust requires an object. You cannot audit, share, version, or stamp a scroll position. When compliance asks "what did the AI tell the analyst and on what basis," the answer must be a document with bound evidence, not 400 lines of transcript exegesis. Chat remains present precisely because it is the best-known instrument for *directing* an agent mid-task — the runner-up becomes a component.
- **Versus notebook:** we keep the notebook's soul (reproducibility — every number traceable to an exact query) and discard its body (cells). The 20% of sessions needing real computation open a sandbox panel *inside* the Inquiry; the artifact stays claim-centric. If, in field use, analysts consistently outgrow the sandbox panel, notebook export is the graduation path (§4.8), not a redesign.
- **Versus dashboard-first:** dashboards are answers to yesterday's questions. We ship investigation first; pinned live Briefs later become the honest dashboard — one whose every tile still carries chips and ledger lineage.
- **Versus report-only:** tens-of-seconds tasks tolerate — and reward — a steering human. The editable plan and mid-run redirection are where analyst expertise enters the loop cheaply, exactly where Gemini/ChatGPT converged (§1.1).

One more default questioned: **should the exec get a different app?** No. Airbnb's deepest lesson is that host and guest are two very different users *sharing one product*, held together by shared objects (the listing, the review). Splitting surfaces splits truth: the exec's simplified app would inevitably drop the evidence layer, recreating the confident-wrong-answer machine we exist to kill. The exec gets the **same Brief** with a different default depth (the top sheet), and the demo becomes: *"the VP reads the same document the analyst built, evidence included."*

## 3. The Lenses — Principles with Testable Implications

*Rule: no homage without an implication. Every principle names a check that can pass or fail.*

### 3.1 Airbnb school — trust between strangers, designed

**A1. Trust is manufactured from structured disclosure, at the right dose.** Gebbia: too little disclosure and too much both collapse acceptance; the interface must *shape* the dose ([TED](https://www.ted.com/talks/joe_gebbia_how_airbnb_designs_for_trust)). → *Implication:* evidence arrives in three fixed doses — chip (1 second), source list on hover (10 seconds), provenance panel on click (10 minutes). **Test:** in usability sessions, ≥90% of participants can state a claim's reliability tier from the chip alone, without opening the panel.

**A2. Reputation must beat similarity.** Users initially trust what feels familiar (a confident paragraph); the system must make *track record and evidence* the visible basis of trust instead. → *Implication:* the Brief's visual hierarchy ranks evidence density above prose fluency — a Corroborated claim in plain words outranks an eloquent Inferred one typographically (placement, weight). **Test:** in a two-claim forced-choice (fluent-Inferred vs. plain-Corroborated), ≥80% of first-week users pick the corroborated claim as "the one I'd put in a memo."

**A3. Content design is the trust system.** Airbnb's breakthroughs were words (review prompts, disclosure nudges) as much as screens. → *Implication:* a versioned **voice-and-vocabulary spec** ships as a v1 engineering artifact: the four confidence words, the refusal template, banned-word list (see §4.4) — enforced by an output linter, not by hope. **Test:** the linter blocks 100% of banned confidence-performing adverbs in answer prose; cloze comprehension tests of the four trust words score ≥90% correct across both audiences.

**A4. Two users, one home.** Host/guest duality: the analyst *produces* trust (builds, verifies, authorizes); the exec *consumes* it. → *Implication:* every Brief renders as a top sheet (headline, ≤5 claims, chips, one chart) that expands into full mechanics; sharing grants the same object with reader defaults. **Test:** an exec-profile participant answers "what's the answer, and how solid is it?" correctly within 10 seconds of opening a shared Brief; the producing analyst finds the SQL within 5 seconds of the same screen.

### 3.2 Anthropic school — how humans should experience AI reasoning

**N1. Calibrated trust, not maximal trust.** The product succeeds when users rely on strong claims *and decline to rely on weak ones* (Lee & See's appropriate reliance; PAIR's "calibrating trust"). → *Implication:* planted-error detection is a release gate (§6): if users accept Inferred-tier errors at high rates, the trust language has failed and the release is blocked — the same way a failing crash test blocks a car.

**N2. Show the work, not the soul.** Tool calls, retrieved records, and executed SQL are audit-true; chain-of-thought is not faithful (F5). → *Implication:* the Work log displays only verifiable events (tool + object + outcome + timestamp); any model-generated reasoning summary is visually labeled "narrative — not evidence" and excluded from the Brief's evidentiary sections. **Test:** an auditor given only the Work log and Ledger reconstructs every asserted fact's basis with zero reliance on reasoning text.

**N3. A refusal must preserve the relationship — by being structural, specific, and useful.** Policy is machine-enforced below the model; the agent should never perform personal moral discomfort on the bank's behalf. → *Implication:* the refusal template has three mandatory slots — [what was blocked] [the named, linked policy] [the compliant alternative, pre-drafted] — and an institutional voice ("Bank policy PII-4 blocks decrypting this field" — never "I'm not comfortable"). **Test:** ≥70% of refusal events are followed by continued work in the same Inquiry (alternative accepted or query reformulated) rather than abandonment.

**N4. Confidence display is an ethical act.** Displaying more certainty than the evidence supports is the product lying; verbalized numeric confidence is known-broken (F6). → *Implication:* confidence appears **only** as the four evidence-derived words + source structure; prose is voice-neutral; "I don't know" is a designed answer layout with a next step (the query that would find out). **Test:** grep-level audit — zero numeric confidence expressions in any shipped surface; the "unknown" state appears in ≥1 of the three onboarding example Briefs (we *teach* the ceiling early).

### 3.3 Apple school — universal comprehensibility

**P1. The first 60 seconds are the whole ballgame.** No manual, no tour; the empty state is the tutorial. → *Implication:* three completed example Briefs (real semantic layer, real chips, one containing an honest Inferred claim and one containing a refusal-with-alternative) are the landing state; first user question is reachable in one action. **Test:** ≥90% of first-time users reach a real answer in under 60 seconds with zero documentation or human help; time-to-first-question median <25s.

**P2. Progressive disclosure with honest depth.** Simplicity that amputates capability is dishonesty of another kind; conceal complexity, don't remove it (the Bloomberg discipline wearing Apple clothes). → *Implication:* every surface passes the glance test at three depths — 1s (headline + chips), 10s (claims), 10min (SQL, lineage, ledger) — and nothing shown at depth 3 contradicts depth 1. **Test:** depth-consistency review in design QA: for 20 sampled Briefs, no claim's chip overstates what its provenance panel supports.

**P3. Direct manipulation over dialogue.** You should not have to *ask* for what you can *touch*. → *Implication:* every entity in agent prose — table, column, metric, code value, policy — is a live object: hover card, click-through to Catalog, draggable into the ask bar. Asking "what's the lineage of LOAN_MASTER?" and clicking the table token are the same operation. **Test:** by week 2, ≥30% of catalog navigations bypass the thread entirely (measured), demonstrating the noun-graph carries real weight.

**P4. One primary action per moment.** Ruthless reduction where it counts: ask → steer → authorize → read. → *Implication:* the Gate has exactly two primary actions (Run / Cancel) plus one secondary (Edit SQL); the Brief has one headline; the workspace has one ask bar. **Test:** heuristic audit — no screen offers more than one primary CTA; Gate error-clicks (authorize-then-immediately-cancel) <2%.

### 3.4 The Eastern European engineering standard (JetBrains/Skype lineage)

**E1. The UI never pretends.** No fake progress, no percentage bars over unbounded work, no optimistic rendering of unconfirmed executions, no skeleton theater. → *Implication:* the working view shows only real, completed tool events and an honest count-up timer; a run is "done" only when its results are bound. **Test:** honesty audit checklist in every design review; zero UI states that assert unverified success (the "agent said it did it" bug class is structurally impossible because completion state derives from the ledger, not from model output).

**E2. Density is earned, and keyboard is a contract.** Analysts get Bloomberg-grade density behind progressive disclosure, and every action has a binding. → *Implication:* full keyboard spec ships in v1 (⌘K ask; j/k claim navigation; e expand evidence; ⌘⇧Enter authorize — a deliberate chord, never bare Enter); compact mode for Brief and Work log; published latency budgets: local interactions <100ms, ask acknowledgment <1s, first real tool event visible <3s. **Test:** an analyst completes ask→steer→authorize→read entirely without the mouse; latency budgets enforced in CI perf tests.

**E3. Mechanics one keystroke away.** Every derived number is traceable to the exact query, profile, or metadata record that produced it — the machine truth is never more than one disclosure level down. → *Implication:* "no naked numbers": every figure in Brief prose is a link to its origin; every chart's underlying result set is inspectable and exportable. **Test:** for any sampled number in any Brief, a reviewer reaches its generating SQL/ledger entry in ≤2 clicks.

**E4. Anti-ornament.** Every pixel encodes state or it doesn't ship; motion only communicates state change; the palette is semantic. → *Implication:* one accent hue; chip fill-level carries the confidence ordinal; red is reserved exclusively for system failure (never for low confidence — low confidence is honest information, not an error); no confetti, streaks, mascots, or celebration states anywhere in a credit-risk tool. **Test:** design-token audit — every color token maps to a named semantic; zero decorative animation tokens.

## 4. THE VISION

### 4.1 Objects and information architecture

Five nouns. Everything in the product is one of them.

```
Workspace
 ├── Inquiry (the case)                      ← unit of work & of audit context
 │    ├── Thread        (steering: user ↔ agent turns; transient rank)
 │    ├── Brief         (the artifact: claims + evidence; durable, versioned, shareable)
 │    ├── Work log      (real tool events: what the agent actually did)
 │    └── Gates         (authorization records for this inquiry)
 ├── Catalog (the governed landscape: tables, columns, metrics, code values,
 │            owners, lineage — browsable without the agent)
 └── Ledger (global, filterable audit surface: every execution, every authorization,
             each linking back to its Inquiry and Brief)
```

- **Workspace (home).** The ask bar front and center (also summoned anywhere via ⌘K), my Inquiries with live status chips, Briefs shared with me, and — for analysts — a right rail of recently touched tables/metrics. Zero-state: the three example Briefs (§4.6).
- **Inquiry.** Two panes: thread left, Brief right, with the Work log as a bottom drawer (analyst default: visible and compact; reader default: collapsed). URL-addressable; async-safe — an Inquiry keeps working when you leave and shows `working / needs authorization / done / failed` in the workspace list. The Inquiry, not the message, is the unit compliance reasons about.
- **Brief anatomy (top to bottom):**
  1. **Headline** — one plain-English sentence. The answer.
  2. **Claims** — short assertions, each ending in its evidence chip. Never a wall of prose; claims are list-structured and individually addressable (deep-linkable, challengeable, quotable).
  3. **Exhibits** — result tables, charts (each chart titled by its claim, sourced by its query), definitions used.
  4. **Basis** — the SQL that ran (with plain-English annotation), tables touched with sensitivity tags, assumptions the agent made (explicitly listed — every silent assumption is a future incident), and open unknowns.
  5. **How we know this** — the auto-generated provenance appendix: every claim → its evidence records → their source classes. Free for the analyst; gold for the reviewer.
  6. **Ledger stamp** — who authorized which executions, when, with SQL hashes. Travels with every export.
- **Catalog.** The semantic layer as a first-class browsable surface — because the agent must not be the only door to the landscape. Every entity token anywhere in the product opens the same entity card: definition, owner, lineage graph, code values, profile stats, *and the same chips* (a column description sourced from official metadata reads Sourced; one inferred by the AI reads Inferred, right in the catalog). One trust language across agent and catalog, or neither is believed.
- **Ledger.** The same audit surface for all three audiences — the analyst self-audits, the reviewer investigates, the exec (rarely) verifies. Transparency is symmetrical: knowing that everyone sees the same ledger is itself a trust mechanic, the way Airbnb's two-sided reviews are.

### 4.2 The interaction model

- **Ask.** Natural language in the ask bar; entity tokens autocomplete as you type (`@LOAN_MASTER`, `#net_charge_off_rate`); structured verbs for power users (`/lineage`, `/define`, `/profile`, `/cost`, `/rerun`) — the palette teaches its own shortcuts inline, Superhuman-style. Prose and command are the same box; progressive disclosure from sentence to syntax.
- **Plan-before-spend.** Quick metadata questions just run. Anything projected >15s or requiring execution posts a **plan** first — 3–6 real steps, editable (strike a step, add a constraint), with a projected cost line if warehouse work is likely downstream. Small friction, placed exactly where human judgment is cheapest (§1.1).
- **Tiered autonomy (fixed, not configurable-per-user):**
  - *Read metadata / lineage / definitions* → auto-runs, logged in Work log.
  - *Sandbox computation & charting on already-authorized data* → auto-runs with notice.
  - *Warehouse execution* → **always gated. No exceptions, no "always allow."** The bank's policy makes this mandatory; the design treats it as the product's spine, not a speed bump (see Moment 4 for how we make mandatory feel worthy rather than nagging).
- **Steering mid-flight.** The user can interject at any time; the agent checkpoints, incorporates, and continues — no lost work, ever. `Esc` pauses the run with partial findings intact (EU AI Act Art. 14's interruptibility, as a keyboard shortcut).
- **Everything is an object.** Claims, chips, tables, metrics, gates, ledger entries — hoverable, linkable, draggable into the ask bar as context. Dialogue is one instrument; the noun-graph is the other.

### 4.3 The seven defining moments

**Moment 1 — First contact (minute zero).**
A new analyst — or a VP in a boardroom — lands on the workspace. No modal, no tour, no video. Three completed example Inquiries sit there, answered against the bank's actual semantic layer: one definitional (*"What officially counts as a default in our book?"* — Sourced/Corroborated chips), one investigative (*"Why do FICO bands differ between RETAIL_SCORE and BUREAU_FEED?"* — includes an honest **Inferred** claim and an open unknown), one that hit a policy rail (*"Show me example account records"* — a refusal with its compliant alternative visibly taken). Hovering any chip opens the source list; the whole trust system is learned by touching it once. Under the ask bar, one line of copy does the calibration work: **"Every answer shows its evidence. Start with something you already know."** — engineering the first session so the user *verifies us against themselves* and catches both our strength and our limits on day one. (Airbnb A1/A2; Apple P1; Anthropic N4 — the Inferred chip and the refusal are deliberately in the first minute. A trust system demonstrated only on its best behavior is a brochure.)

**Moment 2 — The wait (30–90 seconds of real work).**
The plan posts, then executes as a live timeline of *real* events: `Read schema: LOAN_MASTER — 84 columns · 0:04`, `Lineage: CHARGE_OFF_AMT ← GL_FEED.WRITEOFF · 0:11`, `Drafting SQL — cost estimate pending · 0:19`. Intermediate findings pin as cards the user can already read. An honest count-up timer; a step counter only when steps are real. If the projection exceeds ~20 seconds: **"About a minute. You can leave — this keeps working."** The Inquiry's status chip in the workspace carries the state; completion notifies once (quiet-dark: annunciate only what needs a human). This is Buell & Norton's operational transparency with the EE constraint applied: *we get the labor illusion's trust dividend only because it isn't an illusion.* Waiting becomes the first act of evidence disclosure — by the time the answer lands, the user has already watched its provenance being gathered. Reduced-motion users get the same timeline without pulse animation; screen readers get throttled polite announcements per completed step, not per token.

**Moment 3 — The answer lands (composed, not streamed).**
The Brief materializes **atomically**: headline, claims with chips, exhibits — whole. We never stream answer prose. Three reasons, in order of importance: (1) *evidence binding* — a claim physically cannot render before its evidence chip is attached, making the F1 failure class (assert first, source later or never) structurally impossible; (2) *anchoring* — users judge and quote half-formed sentences, and fluent-but-unfinished text reads as confidence the system hasn't earned (fluency is exactly the miscalibrated signal, §1.2); (3) *the read is the product* — an exec glancing for one second must meet a finished document, not a typewriter. The perceived-latency cost is paid by Moment 2: the work was the progress indicator. The three-depth read now runs: 1 second — headline + worst chip in view; 10 seconds — the claims; 10 minutes — Basis, provenance appendix, ledger stamp. And when the evidence isn't there, the layout has a designed **"Not established"** state: what we looked for, what we found instead, and the pre-drafted query that would settle it — "I don't know" with a next step attached (aviation's alert+procedure).

**Moment 4 — The Gate (authorization as a signing ceremony).**
The agent has drafted warehouse SQL. Execution is mandatory-human, audited, costed — so the moment is designed as the most information-dense card in the product, worth reading every single time:

> **Run against warehouse?**
> *Intent:* Count Q2 charge-offs by state, retail portfolio only.
> *SQL:* `SELECT state, COUNT(*) … ` (expandable, syntax-lit, **editable** — with plain-English annotation per clause)
> *Blast panel:* est. cost **$0.42** · scans ~2.1M rows · returns ≤10,000 (capped) · est. 14s
> *Touches:* `LOAN_MASTER` (Confidential), `GEO_REF` (Internal)
> *Policy:* PII-4, ROW-CAP-2 checks passed — nothing sensitive leaves masked form
> *Ledger:* this run is recorded — you, now, SQL hash, linked to this Inquiry
> **[ Run — logged ]** **[ Edit SQL ]** **[ Cancel ]**

Keyboard: **⌘⇧Enter** to authorize — a deliberate chord; bare Enter never authorizes anything (no accidental signatures). Declining is free and unremarked: Cancel returns to the thread with the draft preserved, and the agent proposes cheaper alternatives (sandbox estimate, narrower scope) without pleading. **Anti-habituation is designed, not hoped for** (F4): gates are rare (only executions gate); re-run variants render as *diffs against the last authorized query* so the human reads only what changed; and a user may grant **scoped session consent** — "similar reads, same tables, same caps, next 30 minutes" — which batches consent at the granularity of *risk* while every individual run still lands on the ledger. Edit-before-run is deliberately prominent: Dietvorst's modifiability effect means the ability to nudge the SQL is what keeps analysts using the system after they see it err. We also instrument the ceremony itself: median gate dwell time is a tracked health metric — if it collapses toward reflexive clicking, the gate gets redesigned, not louder.

**Moment 5 — The refusal (institutional, structural, useful).**
The user asks for decrypted PII. The response is three slots, always in this order, in the bank's voice rather than a persona's:

> **Blocked by policy.** Decrypting `SSN_ENC` is prevented by **PII-4** (machine-enforced below this assistant — I could not run this even in error). → *link to the actual policy text*
> **What I can do:** cohort counts on the masked field, or a match-rate analysis that never exposes values. First one is drafted — run it?

No apology theater, no "as an AI I'm not comfortable" — the rail is the bank's, it is structural, and saying so *builds* trust in every non-refused answer (if the rails are real, then what passed the rails is real too). The compliant alternative is mandatory whenever one exists — a refusal is a redirect, not a wall (aviation: no alert without a procedure; Airbnb: recovery language that preserves the relationship). Refusal events append to the Inquiry's record like everything else: compliance sees not just that the rail held, but that the user was routed somewhere legitimate. Continuation-after-refusal is a tracked product metric with a target (§6).

**Moment 6 — The repair (being wrong, gracefully).**
An analyst knows the Q2 number is off. Every claim carries a quiet **Challenge** affordance: "This looks wrong because…". The agent's response protocol is fixed: (1) restate what the claim rests on (its actual evidence records); (2) *propose the cheapest verification* — usually a drafted, costed query — rather than defend itself; (3) if the user is right, issue a **visible correction**: the claim is struck-through-and-replaced in the Brief (never silently mutated — the Brief is versioned, and anyone holding a shared link sees "revised" with the delta); (4) offer to promote the correction, with the analyst's attribution, into the curated-knowledge source — so the fix compounds and the substrate's "historical analyst knowledge" channel grows from real use. This moment is designed *because* Dietvorst says one visible error is where algorithm trust usually dies — unless the human has agency in the fix. Here, being wrong is choreographed: the system loses the point and wins the relationship. (And per F2: the agent concedes on *evidence*, never on social pressure — a challenge without grounds gets the same protocol, and step 2's verification query sometimes proves the *user* wrong, politely, with receipts.)

**Moment 7 — The handoff (trust that travels).**
The analyst's Brief goes to a VP before a risk committee; the VP forwards it onward. Export (PDF, link, deck-paste) always carries: the headline and claims *with chips rendered* (fill-glyphs survive grayscale printing and PowerPoint paste), the "How we know this" appendix, and the ledger stamp. A shared link opens the same Brief in reader depth — top sheet first, everything else one disclosure down; the reader can hover the same chips the analyst hovered. **No naked numbers leave the building**: strip-the-evidence is not an export option, because a number divorced from its provenance is precisely the artifact that destroys institutional trust three meetings later. This is also the compliance story completed: the reviewer's "fire drill" — who ran what, why, authorized by whom — is answerable from any exported Brief's stamp back through the Ledger in minutes.

### 4.4 The trust language (full specification)

**The four words.** Mapped 1:1 onto the substrate's calibrated levels and source classes; used identically in Briefs, hovers, Catalog, Ledger, exports, and speech (screen readers):

| Chip | Word | Meaning | Substrate mapping |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⬤⬤⬤ | **Corroborated** | Independent source classes agree | multi-source corroborated |
| ⬤⬤◯ | **Sourced** | One authoritative source | official metadata, or curated domain knowledge |
| ⬤◯◯ | **Reported** | Observed in practice, not authoritative | data profiling; historical analyst queries |
| ◌◌◌ (dashed) | **Inferred** | AI belief; no independent source | AI inference |

**Display rules (normative):**
1. **Words, never percentages.** Numeric confidence implies a calibration the display cannot honestly guarantee (F6). Counts are allowed — "3 sources" — because counts are facts.
2. **Chip at the claim, not the paragraph.** Per NN/g, the source sits beside the assertion it supports; one paragraph may legitimately carry three different chips.
3. **Never color alone.** The ordinal is carried by word + fill-glyph; hue is constant; **Inferred** is additionally dashed/outline. Survives grayscale, colorblindness, photocopies, and PowerPoint. Red is reserved for system failure — low confidence is honest information, not an error state.
4. **Two-speed disclosure.** Hover → source list ("Corroborated — official metadata + profiling + 41 analyst queries"). Click → provenance panel with the actual evidence records, each dated and typed, plus a **Verify this** action that drafts the confirming query. Doubt always has a next step.
5. **Prose stays voice-neutral.** The agent never performs certainty or doubt in words — no "definitely," "certainly," "I'm confident," "probably" in answer prose; the chips carry all of it. Enforced by the output linter (A3). The agent says "the metadata defines X; profiling agrees" — subject-verb-evidence, in plain American English at a general reading level.
6. **The worst chip governs the headline.** A headline synthesized from mixed-tier claims displays the *lowest* contributing tier beside it. Optimistic aggregation is lying by rollup.
7. **Screen-reader grammar.** Chips narrate as "claim — corroborated, three sources," never as unexplained glyphs.
8. **The exec read and the analyst read are the same read.** A VP learns one rule in ten seconds: *filled dots good, dashed circle means the machine is guessing.* An analyst gets the full provenance graph underneath. Same language, different depth — never a separate "simple mode."

**Voice rules (the agent's character):** an instrument, not a colleague. No name, no avatar, no first-person feelings, no exclamation points, no flattery of the question (F2, F3). Competence-warm, not social-warm: warmth is expressed as anticipating the next need (pre-drafted alternatives, "you can leave, this keeps working"), never as chattiness. The agent speaks *for the bank's data estate* — because legally and reputationally, it does (Air Canada).

### 4.5 Waiting, empty, and error states

- **Waiting** — fully specified in Moment 2. Additional rule: never block the workspace; a running Inquiry is one of possibly several, and the list view carries live status. Notifications follow the quiet-dark contract — exactly three triggers: *gate awaiting you*, *run complete*, *run failed*. Zero engagement pings, forever.
- **Empty states teach.** Workspace-empty is the tutorial (Moment 1). Catalog search with no hit: "No table matches 'chrg_off' — closest: `CHARGE_OFF_EVT` (owner: Risk Data Eng)." A Brief with no establishable answer is the designed "Not established" state, not an apology paragraph.
- **Errors never blur three different truths.** The UI structurally distinguishes: **(a) policy denial** ("blocked by PII-4" — a rail, working as intended, with alternative), **(b) system failure** ("warehouse timeout in `wh-prod-3` at step 4 of 6 — partials kept; Retry step") and **(c) true absence** ("query ran; zero rows match — here's why that might be"). Conflating any two of these is how users learn to distrust all three. Failures name the failing subsystem (E1), keep all partial findings, and end with a proposed next action. The agent never announces success it cannot show on the ledger — completion state derives from the ledger, not from model output, so the "agent claims it did it" failure class cannot render.

### 4.6 The first 60 seconds (the full script)

- **0–5s:** Workspace loads under 1s. Ask bar focused, caret blinking. Three example Briefs visible with their status and chips. One line of copy: *"Ask about the data landscape. Every answer shows its evidence."* Nothing else demands attention.
- **5–30s:** The user opens an example Brief (curiosity does the tutorial's job). They see headline → claims → chips; a hover reveals sources; they have now learned 90% of the product's trust system through direct manipulation. The examples deliberately include one Inferred claim, one "Not established," and one refusal-with-alternative — the system's *limits* are in the first minute by design (N1/N4): calibration starts before question one.
- **30–60s:** They ask their own question — typed, or by clicking a suggested question grounded in their actual entitlements. Metadata questions answer in seconds; a longer one posts its plan and teaches Moment 2. Either way: a real answer, with chips, inside the first minute, with zero manual, zero tour, zero training session. (Test targets in §3.3 P1 and §6.)
- The 60-second bar holds for *both* audiences: the analyst's first minute ends in a real answer; the exec's first minute — often inside someone else's shared Brief — ends in a correctly-read one.

### 4.7 Accessibility posture

WCAG 2.2 **AA minimum, AAA where cheap**, treated as an engineering budget, not a checklist pass:

- **Perception.** Confidence never by color alone (§4.4 rule 3); text contrast ≥4.5:1 (AA) with AAA targeted for body text; charts follow accessible-palette discipline with patterned/labeled series; all hover content has click/focus parity (no hover-only information — also the touch story).
- **Operation.** 100% keyboard-completable, including the Gate (⌘⇧Enter chord; focus trap; Esc always cancels); visible focus ≥3:1; target sizes ≥24px; no time-limited decisions — a Gate waits indefinitely and is re-summonable.
- **Understanding.** Trust vocabulary at a broad reading level, cloze-tested across the 25-year-old analyst and the 60-year-old executive (A3); errors in plain language naming the subsystem and the next step; layouts stable — no reflowing content under a moving cursor.
- **Robustness.** Work-log streaming announced via throttled `aria-live="polite"` (per step, never per token); gates announced assertive; Brief semantics are real document semantics (headings, lists, tables) so exports and screen readers get the same structure; 200% zoom without loss; reduced-motion honored with static-equivalent state displays.

### 4.8 What graduates later (v2 / v3)

**v2 — the workspace compounds:** **Monitors** (a Brief re-run on schedule; quiet-dark change alerts only when a claim's value or confidence tier actually moves); multiplayer Inquiries (roles: driver / reviewer / reader) with claim-level comments; Brief templates ("credit memo basis," "model-validation note") that pre-shape claims for downstream documents; the exec weekly digest — an auto-Brief of *what changed in the landscape*, chips included; mobile **read** for Briefs and Ledger (authorization stays desktop-first: first-time scopes require the full Gate; identical-scope re-approval may reach mobile later, with the same dwell instrumentation).
**v3 — the substrate learns, governed:** promotion pipeline maturing analyst corrections (Moment 6) into curated knowledge with review workflow — the Airbnb reputation loop for facts; proactive anomaly annunciation (the agent notices a lineage break or metric drift and files an Inquiry *draft* — never an unsolicited execution); what-if sandbox modeling on authorized extracts; headless API so Briefs and chips embed in the credit-memo toolchain; notebook export for the power-analyst tail that outgrows the sandbox panel.
**Never graduates:** un-gated warehouse execution; numeric confidence; a persona. These are load-bearing walls, not v1 austerity.

## 5. What NOT to Build

1. **A persona.** No name, face, feelings, or first-person charm. Anthropomorphic design measurably inflates trust while deflecting institutional responsibility ([Collabra](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/12/1/161757/218332/The-Effects-of-AI-Anthropomorphism-on-Trust-and)); the bank is liable for every sentence regardless ([Air Canada / Moffatt](https://www.forbes.com/sites/marisagarcia/2024/02/19/what-air-canada-lost-in-remarkable-lying-ai-chatbot-case/)). A mascot in a credit-risk tool is a liability with a smile.
2. **Percentage confidence, gauges, or "AI trust scores."** Verbalized/numeric LLM confidence is systematically miscalibrated ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02917); [Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01217-9)); PAIR itself warns confidence displays can mislead ([PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)). Our evidence-structural words are the honest alternative.
3. **Chain-of-thought as explanation.** Reasoning traces are unfaithful (~25% hint acknowledgment — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-faithfulness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoning)). Tool events and retrieved records are the explanation; reasoning summaries are labeled narrative. Watching the model "think" is theater; watching it *work* is audit.
4. **Per-action consent nagging — or its evil twin, "always allow."** CDS overrides at 90–95% ([JMIR](https://medinform.jmir.org/2020/7/e15653)) and UAC habituation ([BeyondTrust](https://www.beyondtrust.com/blog/entry/user-account-control-best-practices)) show both poles fail: prompts as noise, or safety signed away wholesale. Build the rare, dense, diff-based, scope-consented Gate instead.
5. **Streamed answer prose.** Token-streaming optimizes perceived latency ([TTFT](https://medium.com/@sonitanishk2003/streaming-responses-how-to-build-snappy-real-time-llm-ux-00a92dbb7b3e)) at the cost of anchoring users on unfinished, unevidenced text — fluency masquerading as confidence, the exact signal known to be miscalibrated. Stream the work (real events); compose the answer.
6. **An auto-generated dashboard/insights feed.** "AI insights" sprawl is the fastest route to F7/F8: unasked-for charts nobody trusts, pinned by nobody, audited by nobody ([Power BI Copilot's record](https://colrows.com/blogs/power-bi-copilot-wrong-answers/); [MIT GenAI Divide](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)). Dashboards arrive in v2 only as *pinned Briefs* that kept their chips and stamps.
7. **A separate executive app.** Splitting surfaces splits truth (§2.3). The "simple" fork will drop the evidence layer within two quarters and become the confident-wrong-answer machine. Projection, not product.
8. **Chat as system of record.** Transcripts are steering exhaust. If it matters, it is in the Brief, the Work log, or the Ledger — addressable, versioned, exportable ([Wattenberger](https://wattenberger.com/thoughts/boo-chatbots/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/ai/)).
9. **Engagement mechanics.** No streaks, badges, celebration confetti, usage nudges, or "you haven't asked me anything lately" (quiet-dark: three notification triggers, ever). Sycophancy began as an engagement metric ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/)); in this domain, retention comes from being right and honest.
10. **Voice/avatar interfaces, and out-of-scope general chat.** The agent answers the governed landscape; beyond it, it says so plainly ("outside my data landscape") rather than freelancing on world knowledge — scope honesty is what makes in-scope authority credible (Amershi G1/G2: make clear what the system can do, and how well).

## 6. Validation Plan

*Philosophy: we are not validating whether people like it; we are validating whether trust lands **calibrated**. Cheap methods, real analysts, real executives, pre-registered thresholds. RITE-style iteration between rounds.*

**Phase 0 — Language before pixels (week 1–2; ~15 participants; cloze + card sort).**
Test the four trust words and refusal template as *text artifacts* with 8 analysts and 7 exec-profile readers. Cloze comprehension ("a claim marked Reported means ___") and rank-ordering of the four tiers. **Gate: ≥90% correct tier-ordering across both audiences**; any word scoring below that is replaced before any screen is built. (Fixing vocabulary costs nothing now and everything later — content design is the trust system, A3.)

**Phase 1 — Wizard-of-Oz Inquiries (week 3–5; 6–8 working analysts, their own real questions).**
A researcher-driven mock (real semantic-layer metadata, human behind the curtain for agent turns) runs the full loop: ask → plan → wait → Brief → Gate → repair. Measures: time-to-first-verified-answer; evidence-open rate (% of Briefs where the user opened ≥1 provenance panel before saying they'd act); Gate comprehension (think-aloud: can they state cost, scope, and ledger consequence before authorizing — **target 100%**); moment-of-doubt observation (what do they do when they disbelieve a claim — the Challenge affordance must be discovered unprompted by **≥60%**).

**Phase 2 — Trust-calibration experiments (week 5–8; the release gates).** Seeded-error protocol: instrumented prototype sessions where planted claims are wrong at known tiers (disclosed in debrief; ethics-reviewed). Three **measurable trust-calibration outcomes**, pre-registered:

1. **Reliance gap (the headline metric).** Acceptance rate ("would you put this claim in a memo?") of correct-Corroborated claims minus acceptance of planted-error-Inferred claims. **Target: ≥40 points** (e.g., ≥90% vs ≤50%), with users catching **≥75% of planted Inferred-tier errors** while retaining **≥90% acceptance of correct Corroborated claims**. A flat acceptance curve across tiers means the chips are decoration and the release is blocked (N1). (Operationalizes Lee & See's appropriate reliance.)
2. **The 10-second executive read.** Exec-profile participants view a shared Brief for 10 seconds, then rank its three key claims by reliability and state the headline. **Target: ≥80% correct rank-order, ≥90% correct headline recall.** This is the entire host/guest thesis in one number.
3. **Refusal continuation.** After a scripted policy refusal: **≥70%** of sessions continue productively in the same Inquiry (accept the alternative or reformulate) versus abandon; post-task trust rating of the *system overall* must not drop after a well-formed refusal (a refusal that costs global trust is mis-written, N3).

**Phase 3 — Field pilot instrumentation (first 90 days with ~20 analysts + their leadership chain).**
Longitudinal health metrics with alert thresholds: **Gate dwell time** (median across *novel* gates must stay ≥3s through week 8 — collapse toward reflexive clicking triggers a gate redesign, F4); **verification efficiency curve** (evidence-opens per acted-on Brief should *fall for Corroborated* and *hold for Inferred* over time — efficient trust, not blind trust); **"Not established" rate** (if the agent never says it, calibration is broken upstream — floor ≥2% of Briefs); **correction promotion rate** (Moment 6 corrections accepted into curated knowledge — the compounding-trust flywheel); **audit fire drill** (quarterly: a real compliance reviewer reconstructs a randomly sampled execution — who, what, why, authorized-by — from Ledger + Brief alone in **<5 minutes, unaided**); and the two-question trust survey, monthly: *"the system is honest with me about what it knows"* (want: high and rising) vs. *"the system is usually right"* (want: moderate and stable). **Divergence between those two is the product working.**
