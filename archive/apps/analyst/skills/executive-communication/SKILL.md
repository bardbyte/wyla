# Executive communication — the answer is a decision aid

Senior readers allocate attention, not analysis time. The unit of value
is a defensible headline they can repeat in their next meeting.

## Structure (in order, no exceptions)

1. **Headline** — one sentence, number inside it, direction + magnitude +
   period: "Consumer C-30 roll rate rose 14 bps to 2.31% in June — third
   consecutive monthly increase, still 19 bps under risk appetite."
2. **So what** — the business translation, quantified: "At the current
   balance base that's ≈ $46M of additional balances entering early
   delinquency per month." If you computed this in the sandbox, show the
   arithmetic in one parenthetical.
3. **Why (max 3 bullets)** — drivers ranked by contribution, each with
   its number. Rate vs mix decomposition when the skill library covers it.
4. **Watch / decide** — what would change the read, and the decision this
   informs ("if July exceeds 2.4%, vintage-level line management review").
5. **Trust line** — one sentence: confidence tier of the load-bearing
   facts, data freshness, any failing DQ rule. Never bury a caveat that
   would embarrass the number in a bigger meeting.

## Language rules

- Plain terms over internal codes: "accounts moving from current to 30
  days past due", not "C→30 event-basis migration". First use of any
  necessary term gets a 5-word gloss.
- Absolute numbers with the rate: "2.31% (~$310M of $13.4B)".
- Round to the precision that survives a hallway retelling (bps for
  rates, $M for money) — the exact figures live in the appendix table.
- Active, declarative, no hedging stacks: one clearly-labeled uncertainty
  beats three "may/might/could"s.
- Never present a `guessed`-tier fact to an executive as fact — either
  corroborate it first or present it as an open question.

## Visual pairing

C-suite: headline + at most ONE visual (stat tile, or single-series line
with the threshold annotated in the caption). VP: stat row + trend +
driver bar dashboard. The chart repeats the headline's number — a reader
who only sees the chart must reach the same conclusion.

## The test

Before sending: would a smart, busy person with no context (a) get the
point in 10 seconds, (b) know what it means for the business, and
(c) know whether to trust it? If any answer is no, restructure.
