# Critique Panel — Radix Brief Workspace (mock v2)

**Object:** `apps/console/design/radix-workspace-mock.html` (v2), read against mock v1 (`console-mock.html`), the clean-room vision (`greenfield-vision.md`), the prior expert evaluation (`ux-research-notes.md`, 27 findings — assumed to land; not repeated here), and the synthesis (`synthesis-vision.md`).
**Panel:** three voices, three schools. The Airbnb-school designer (trust-between-strangers as a designed system), the Robinhood-school designer (radical simplification of an intimidating domain, plus the regulatory scar tissue), and Rory Sutherland (behavioral science / Ogilvy, as published in *Alchemy*, TED, The Spectator).
**Method note:** this is an anchored round — the panel saw everything. Disagreements between panelists are preserved deliberately; where they conflict, both positions are recorded and, where possible, converted into an A/B question. Panelist names denote schools of practice, not endorsements by the individuals or companies.
**Date:** 2026-07-07.

---

## 1. Verdicts

### The Airbnb-school designer

The strongest thing in v2 is the projection architecture. Brief / Thread / Ledger as three renderings of one object is the host–guest lesson applied correctly: two very different user classes, one shared artifact, and no forked "simple mode" that would shed the evidence layer within two quarters. The Brief is a listing page for a fact — artifact-first, claims individually addressable, repair designed in. Those are the bones of a trust marketplace.

The most dangerous weakness: **nothing accumulates.** Trust between strangers is built from visible history, and this interface has none. Briefs don't age, get verified, or get cited; the agent's only identity is a vendor model name in a pill ("Gemini 3.1 Pro · Vertex") — a host profile listing the brand of their car. The org will run planted-error calibration tests as release gates and then keep the results in a drawer. And the second user — the reader, the VP, the *guest* — has zero pixels in this mock. Airbnb without reviews and without a guest side is Craigslist with better photography. One-directional, moment-in-time trust is exactly the "brochure" the vision itself warned against.

### The Robinhood-school designer

The strongest thing in v2 is the discipline of the consequential moment. Hold-to-approve instead of Enter; the answer landing whole instead of streaming (streaming is the typewriter dopamine of AI products); four words instead of confidence scores. This team internalized, without having lived it, the lesson that cost us the confetti (removed March 2021 under gamification scrutiny) and a record $70M FINRA penalty (June 2021): engagement mechanics have no business near decisions that can hurt people. The topsheet genuinely passes the one-glance test.

The most dangerous weakness: **complexity got organized, not simplified — and the semantic system lies under stress.** Eighteen-odd nouns before lunch; three panes a 1080px screen already can't hold (the sidebar deletes itself); no phone story although the exec's brief will be opened from an email in an elevator. Worse, two live bombs: the green up-arrow delta grammar will cheerfully celebrate a delinquency spike, and the sidebar says "Composing" while the system is actually stopped, waiting on your signature. The two most consequential states in the product, both mislabeled. In my world that isn't polish debt — that's the exhibit stapled to the regulator's complaint.

### Rory Sutherland

The strongest thing here is the hold. The entire software industry is sanding friction off everything it touches, and this product has made a deliberately costly gesture its spine — and costliness is precisely why it will be believed. A signature that costs nothing signals nothing (*Alchemy*: signaling must be expensive to be credible). Second prize: the planted Inferred claim in the hero brief — the blemish that sells the rest (the pratfall/blemishing effect). The mock's smartest psychological decision, possibly made by accident.

The most dangerous weakness: **the product is honest to machines and mute to humans.** GB scanned, 0.93 to two decimal places, 0.7 seconds, mm:ss — units nobody can feel, precision nobody can use. Meanwhile its genuinely persuasive assets — the ledger (a notary!), the wait (corroboration you can watch!), the refusal (the best advertisement the product owns) — are treated as plumbing, timekeeping, and an edge case. V2 has perfect table manners and no sense of occasion. The end of an inquiry is a beige line, and ends are what people remember (peak–end rule).

## 3. How we would have mocked it

### The Airbnb-school designer

1. **A second browser frame: the guest side.** The demo line is "the VP reads the same document the analyst built" — so mock the VP's screen: a shared-link reader view with reader chrome, hover-openable chips, "shared by S. Gandhi · viewed by 3," and a reader-initiated question/challenge affordance (guests review too). One frame, and the handoff stops being a toast.
2. **Sidebar items as listing cards.** Title + state is a file manager. A listing card carries its trust summary: "3 Corroborated · 1 Inferred · re-run Jul 3 · cited twice." Scannable reputation at the shelf, before you open anything.
3. **Storyboard the return, not just the run.** We storyboarded the *frames* of a trip before designing screens (the 2012 storyboarding exercise); the missing frame here is 9:04 a.m.: two briefs landed overnight, one waits on your signature, one claim got challenged by a teammate. The vision promises "you can leave — this keeps working"; the mock never shows what coming back feels like. (Distinct from the prior study's in-flight findings — this is re-entry, a different trip stage.)

**Endorse as-is:** the projections-over-persona-toggle move (v1's "Executive" label was rank flattery; "same object, different depth" is the host/guest lesson done right); the four-word vocabulary as content design; the Challenge → cheapest-verification repair loop — trust is built at repair, and v2 made repair a first-class affordance rather than a support ticket.

### The Robinhood-school designer

1. **Phone frame first.** The 390px Card projection next to the desktop frame, plus the gate-as-notification and the designed desk-cliff ("signature waits at your desk"). If the topsheet doesn't survive an elevator, the topsheet isn't done.
2. **One-decision gate.** The current gate card stacks eight informational objects around one decision (intent, SQL, four pills, hold, keyboard note, diff rule). Mock it as: intent sentence + blast radius + hold. Everything else behind one "Details" disclosure. Ruthless progressive disclosure is not hiding — the SQL is one tap away, exactly where an analyst will look and a VP won't. *(Sutherland dissent, recorded: density is part of the ceremony's cost; a gate that reads in two seconds will be signed in two seconds. Resolution proposal: collapse for low tiers, full density for high tiers — which also serves R6.)*
3. **Ship the "never delightful" spec as token law.** The mock is clean today because this team has taste; encode it before a growth PM arrives: gate, refusal, deterioration deltas, ledger, and error states get zero motion budget and zero valence color. Delight budget lives only in input affordances, typography, and the example briefs' craft. Write the sentence into the token file: *"The signature stays boring forever."*

**Endorse as-is:** hold-to-approve (the anti-confetti — deliberate friction at the consequential act, the exact inversion of what we were fined for); the answer landing whole (refusing the streaming dopamine is the same discipline as removing the confetti); four words, no percentages — numbers perform confidence, and performed confidence is what regulators eventually subpoena.

### Rory Sutherland

1. **The visible self-attack pass.** After composing, the agent spends 5–10 seconds *trying to break its own brief*, on camera: "re-derived the June count via an alternate join — matched · checked the campaign hypothesis against approval-channel mix — still unproven." The wait becomes corroboration you can watch (Buell & Norton's operational transparency, used honestly — and it literally mints the Corroborated chips the product sells).
2. **Pace the consequential answer.** Never let a signed warehouse answer land in under ~8 seconds *even when it could*. Instant authority reads as guesswork; the felt experience of a wait, not its duration, is the design material — "the Uber map is a psychological moonshot: it does not reduce the waiting time, it makes waiting 90% less frustrating" (*Alchemy*). Same move, inverted: don't shorten the wait, *spend* it. The floor is filled with the self-attack pass, so it is real work, never padding — the engineering-honesty rule survives. And an identical claim delivered with visible effort is believed more (expensive-placebo effect: Waber, Shiv, Carmon & Ariely 2008; Plassmann et al. 2008). *(RH dissent, recorded: never add latency; Sutherland's reply: I'm not adding latency, I'm refusing to amputate verification to win a speed contest no one asked for.)*
3. **Refusal-first onboarding.** Order the example set so the first thing a new user opens is the refusal-with-alternative. The fastest way to be believed is to be seen refusing; a visible constraint is the cheapest credible advertisement (costly signaling: the short menu signals the fresh kitchen). Neither of my colleagues would ever lead with a "no" — which is precisely why it will work. *(Airbnb dissent: first contact should build belonging before boundaries; run it as the Phase-1 A/B.)*

**Endorse as-is:** the planted Inferred claim in the hero brief (the blemishing effect — Ein-Gar, Shiv & Tormala 2012 — one honest flaw inoculates the rest); the ledger-as-receipt grammar (mono stamps everywhere — receipts signal seriousness; "this conversation is notarized" is the product's real positioning against ChatGPT); the hold, directionally — currently underpriced (R6).

## 4. Additional inputs — net-new to the design

Each candidate from the brief, accepted or rejected with reasoning; then the panel's own additions.

1. **Bidirectional trust loops — ACCEPT, split in two.** (a) *The agent grades the question* (R14, Sutherland/Airbnb): words only, before spend. (b) *Reuse counts on briefs* — **accept as provenance, reject as social proof.** "Cited in 2 memos" is a verifiable fact and belongs on the brief's record (R8), linking to the citing artifacts. But never rank, badge, or trend by popularity: popularity mechanics near consequential decisions were Robinhood's original sin, and a much-cited wrong brief is a *worse* wrong brief. Provenance, not applause. *(RH veto shaped this acceptance.)*
2. **Mobile/elevator projection — ACCEPT** (R9), including the designed desk-cliff for authorization. The elevator is where the exec actually reads.
3. **Cost framing in human units — ACCEPT with Sutherland's inversion** (R5): the human unit is *accountability* (grain, rows, sensitivity, budget share), not dollars. Dollars can be the psychologically dishonest unit when the sum is trivial — $0.006 under a signature dissolves the ceremony.
4. **The psychology of the wait as a feature — ACCEPT** (self-attack pass + latency floor + the existing work log). Both engineers' schools agree on the constraint: filled time only, never fake time. Dissent on the floor itself is recorded under §3-Sutherland-2.
5. **Brief reputation over time — ACCEPT** (R8): verified-later / challenged-and-survived / revised / re-run / cited-by, plus visible staleness with a one-tap re-run. This is the single largest missing subsystem in v2.
6. **Onboarding as first-trip design — ACCEPT** (R13), with contested ordering: Sutherland's refusal-first vs Airbnb's strength-first. A/B it in the Phase-1 usability rounds; measure first-session trust survey plus later refusal-continuation rate.
7. **What must NEVER be delightful — ACCEPT as a shipped spec** (§3-RH-3): the gate, the refusal, deterioration numbers, the ledger, and error states. One unresolved dissent, kept: **Sutherland's completion "seal."** Peak–end says the inquiry's ending is what memory keeps, and today it's a beige line; he proposes a notarial micro-moment when a brief seals (weight, not celebration — a stamp, never confetti; gravitas *is* the delight for compliance-minded users). RH holds a flat ban: a stamp you start craving is a slot machine with paperwork. **Panel ruling: ship flat in v3; A/B the seal in pilot with dwell-time and reliance-gap guardrails; kill it at the first sign of completion-seeking behavior.**

**Panel additions beyond the brief's candidates:**

8. **The agent's own track record as a surface** (R7) — the calibration release-gate results become the product's Superhost badge instead of an internal QA artifact.
9. **Screenshot/clipboard provenance** (R12) — the informal export paths are the real ones; design them.
10. **Challenge as co-authorship (IKEA effect** — Norton, Mochon & Ariely 2012**).** Credit the challenger in the version line: "v2 — revised after S. Gandhi's challenge." Labor invested raises valuation and transfers ownership — the analyst will *defend* a brief they visibly shaped, which is also the accountability structure the bank wants. Sutherland's colleague-proof extension: occasionally require a human choice mid-composition (which of 3 delinquency definitions?) even when the model could default — assembly increases valuation. *(RH: opposed by default — added steps are abandoned tasks; scope strictly to genuinely ambiguous cases, where the choice is real work, not ritual.)*
11. **REJECTED: dual-control / second-approver ceremony for top-tier queries.** Compliance theater beyond the bank's actual policy; it adds a queue where the vision promises flow, and four-eyes review belongs in the steward workflow, not the gate. Revisit only if policy genuinely requires it.
12. **REJECTED: any numeric "question confidence."** Same miscalibration objection as answer percentages. The question grade is three words — clean / ambiguous / unanswerable as posed — with the reason attached.

## 5. Priority synthesis — the panel's joint top-5 for mock v3 / the React build

1. **Truth-of-state pass (R1 + R2 + R3).** Enforce worst-chip-governs everywhere a rollup renders; split valence from direction in the delta/chart tokens; make "Needs your signature" the loudest, truest state. Rationale: v2's trust language is right, and its most-watched happy path currently breaks it three ways. *No dissent.*
2. **The second user gets pixels (R4 + R9 + R12).** Reader rendering of a shared brief, the phone Card projection with the desk-cliff, screenshot/clipboard honesty. The handoff is where trust travels; today it is a toast. *No dissent on priority; Airbnb leads the reader, RH leads mobile.*
3. **Gate 2.0 (R5 + R6 + R15).** Accountability-unit framing; graduated signature by sensitivity; de-chromed hold. *Dissent recorded:* RH caps ceremony weight (one gesture below the top tier, tight total-time budget); Sutherland holds that underpricing the signature is exactly how it dies of habituation. The tiered design is the compromise both signed.
4. **Reputation layer v1 (R7 + R8).** Brief lifecycle records (verified / challenged / revised / re-run / cited-by, with staleness) plus the system's own calibration profile behind the runner pill. *Dissent: none on substance; RH requires the provenance-not-popularity rule in writing before reuse counts ship.*
5. **First-trip kit (R13 + R14).** Three example inquiries including a "Not established" and the refusal; restored answerability seeds; the question grade before spend. *Dissent: opening order — refusal-first (Sutherland) vs strength-first (Airbnb) — resolved by A/B in Phase-1 rounds.*

*Near-miss:* R10 (witness-panel decimals → count sentences) missed the top-5 on scope, not merit — it is an afternoon of work; do it with #4.
